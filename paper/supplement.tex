\section{Complete specification of the STEAM algorithm}
\label{ssect:steam algorithm spec}

This section specifies the STEAM algorithm at the level of implementation
detail: discretization, boundary handling, and the order of operations.
The physical construction and its motivation are described in the main
text and its appendices; the intent here is that the simulation could be
reproduced exactly from this description together with the main-text
equations. The reference implementation is the \texttt{turbulon-model}
repository, and the full analysis for this paper is contained in the
companion \texttt{turbulon-analysis} repository.

\subsection{Inputs and size classes}

The inputs are: horizontally uniform ensemble mean profiles $\langle h
\rangle_t(z)$ and $\langle q_t \rangle_t(z)$ sampled at vertical spacing
$\Delta z_p$; the spheroscale profile $l_s(z)$; the outer scale $L$; the
horizontal domain size $L_x, L_y$ and finest grid spacing $dx, dy$; the
domain height $L_z$; physical bounds $[\Phi_{\min}, \Phi_{\max}]$ for
each scalar together with the bound-buffer multiple $n_b$ (default 3);
the surface pressure $p_s$; a random seed; sparsity factors $s_x,
s_y, s_z$ (positive integers, default 1); and the cascade density $n_c$
(size classes per octave, positive integer, default 1). The outer scale
must be a power-of-two multiple of $dx$.

Logarithmically spaced size classes are defined as $k_i = L\, 2^{-i/n_c}$
for $i = 0, 1, \dots, n_c\log_2(L/(2dx))$, proceeding from the outer
scale down to the finest class $k = 2dx$, which is Nyquist-sampled by the
output grid. For each $k_i$ the vertical turbulon size is $k_{z,i}(z) =
l_s(z)(k_i / l_s(z))^{H_z}$, evaluated with the local spheroscale;
where the piecewise-isotropic option is selected, $k_{z,i} = k_i$ for
$k_i < l_s(z)$.

\subsection{Normalization}

The outer-scale amplitude profile $C_{\Phi, L}(z)$ is computed for each
scalar $\Phi \in \{h, q_t\}$ from the mean profile, per the main-text
appendix: at each profile level, the Haar fluctuation at the local
vertical outer scale --- the mean of the profile over the upper half of
a $k_{z,L}(z)$-wide window minus the mean over the lower half, with
half-window width at least one profile cell and the profile edge-padded
by the maximum half-width --- is multiplied by the calibration constant $\lambda = 1/R \approx 0.50$
and taken in absolute value, where $R \approx 2.0$ is the mean absolute
vertical Haar fluctuation of a field of unit-amplitude outer-scale
turbulons, computed from the envelope shape and the turbulon packing;
this makes the simulated Haar fluctuation at the outer scale equal that
of the input profile by construction. The scale-dependent amplitudes
follow $C_{\Phi,k}(z) = n_c^{-1/\alpha}\, C_{\Phi,L}(z)(k/L)^{H_h}$
(density compensation per the main-text appendix) and are interpolated
to each size class's vertical grid.

The bound-buffer widths are computed from the same amplitude profiles:
$b_{\Phi,k}(z) = n_b \sum_{k_j \le k} C_{\Phi,j}(z)$, a linear sum of
the mean absolute amplitudes of the current and all smaller size
classes, with each $C_{\Phi,j}$ interpolated to the current class's
vertical grid. (No deterministic ensemble-mean weight is used: the
advective weight is normalized by its realized horizontal mean at each
level, computed during the cascade loop below.)

\subsection{Working grids}

Turbulons of size $k$ need not be resolved at the finest grid spacing,
so each size class works on a coarsened grid with $\Delta x_k =
k/(2s_x)$ and $\Delta y_k = k/(2s_y)$; when $s = 1$ the spacing is half
the turbulon size, Nyquist-sampling the envelope's peak-PSD wavelength.
The horizontal dimensions $n_{x,k} = L_x/\Delta x_k$ and $n_{y,k} =
L_y/\Delta y_k$ are exact integers provided $L_x$ and $L_y$ are integer
multiples of $L$.

The vertical grid requires more care because $k_z(z)$ varies with
height. Cells are accumulated upward from the surface with local
spacing $k_z(k, l_s(z))/(2s_z)$ until the domain top is reached, and
the resulting spacings are then rescaled by a single common factor so
that they tile $L_z$ exactly. For a constant spheroscale this reduces
to a uniform grid at least as fine as the Nyquist target.

\subsection{Cascade loop}

The perturbation fields $\widetilde{h}$ and $\widetilde{q_t}$ are
initialized to zero and the flux field $\mathcal{F}$ to one, on the
coarsest grid. The algorithm then iterates over size classes from large
to small, performing the following sequence of operations at each class.

First, the perturbation and flux fields are trilinearly interpolated
from the previous class's grid to the current one, and the mean profiles
are linearly interpolated to the current vertical grid.

The flux is then advanced by one size class. A sparse field $\gamma$ of
independent extremal ($\beta = -1$) L\'evy $\alpha$-stable variables,
$\alpha = 1.8$, is drawn at the turbulon centers (every $s_x, s_y, s_z$
cells, i.e. every $k/2$ in physical space; no centers lie below the
surface or above the domain top), scaled by
$c_\mathcal{F} = c\, n_c^{-1/\alpha}$, and shifted by the deterministic
constant $c_\mathcal{F}^\alpha/(\alpha-1)$, which enforces $\langle
e^\gamma \rangle = 1$ exactly (the $\alpha$-generalization of the
lognormal $-\sigma^2/2$ shift; derivation below), so the mean flux is
conserved.
% NOTE (2026-07-27): the reference implementation currently evaluates
% this constant by a large fixed-seed Monte Carlo draw (1.2510); the
% closed form is 1/(alpha-1) = 1.25 exactly. Replacing the measured
% constant with the closed form is checklist item 32 (Thomas's call). The flux is updated additively as
$\mathcal{F} \mathrel{+}= \mathfrak{T}_k * [(e^\gamma - 1)\mathcal{F}]$,
clipped at zero, and rescaled by a single scalar restoring the volume
mean the field had before the advance --- a clip-bias correction only
(in a root simulation the entering mean is one). Level means are not
individually constrained: realized layer-mean flux fluctuations are
retained, and mean-flux flatness holds in expectation through the
generator shift.
The signed scalar noise $S_k$ is the increment $(e^\gamma -
1)\mathcal{F}$ evaluated with the entering flux, normalized by the mean
absolute value of $e^\gamma - 1$ over the turbulon centers; it is shared
between $h$ and $q_t$.

% --- shift-constant derivation (self-contained; may move to the main
% appendix, Thomas's call) ---
The shift constant follows in closed form from the generator's
normalization convention. The unit-scale draws $\gamma_0$ are generated
by the Chambers--Mallows--Stuck algorithm in the universal-multifractal
convention,
\begin{equation*}
\gamma_0 = \frac{\sin\!\big(\alpha(\phi-\phi_0)\big)}
{\big[\,|\alpha-1|\cos\phi\,\big]^{1/\alpha}}
\left[\frac{\cos\!\big(\phi-\alpha(\phi-\phi_0)\big)}{W}\right]^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha},
\qquad \phi_0 = -\frac{\pi}{2}\,\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha},
\end{equation*}
with $\phi$ uniform on $(-\pi/2, \pi/2)$, $W$ unit exponential, and $1 <
\alpha \le 2$. A standard maximally skewed ($\beta=-1$) stable variable
$X$ of unit scale satisfies $\ln\langle e^{\theta X}\rangle =
-\theta^\alpha \sec(\pi\alpha/2)$ for $\theta \ge 0$ --- finite because
maximal negative skew places the heavy power-law tail on the side the
exponential suppresses. The draw above differs from $X$ only by the
constant scale factor $\sigma = |\alpha-1|^{-1/\alpha}\,
\big(1+\tan^2(\pi\alpha/2)\big)^{-1/(2\alpha)}$, and since
$(1+\tan^2)^{1/2} = |\sec\,|$, the secants cancel:
\begin{equation*}
\ln\big\langle e^{\theta \gamma_0} \big\rangle =
\frac{\theta^{\alpha}}{\alpha-1}, \qquad \theta \ge 0 .
\end{equation*}
With $\gamma = c_\mathcal{F}\gamma_0$, the shift
$c_\mathcal{F}^\alpha/(\alpha-1)$ therefore gives $\langle
e^{\gamma-\mathrm{shift}}\rangle = 1$ exactly. At $\alpha = 2$ this
convention gives $\gamma_0 \sim N(0, 2)$ and the shift reduces to the
lognormal $-\sigma^2/2$. More generally, $\ln\langle
e^{q(\gamma-\mathrm{shift})}\rangle = (q^\alpha - q)\,
c_\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}/(\alpha-1)$: each size class contributes a second
characteristic function of the universal-multifractal form, and summed
over the $n_c$ classes in an octave the coefficient is
$c^\alpha/(\alpha-1)$ --- independent of the cascade density, consistent
with the $n_c^{-1/\alpha}$ compensation above.
% --- end shift-constant derivation ---

Next, the scalar amplitudes are computed. Gradients of the running field
$f = \langle \Phi \rangle_t + \widetilde{\Phi}$ are taken with periodic
central differences in $x$ and $y$ and with one-sided differences at the
vertical boundaries (central in the interior). The weight $W_{\Phi,k} =
|\nabla_h f| + (k_z/k)|\partial f/\partial z|$ is divided by its
realized horizontal mean at each level (levels where the mean is
exactly zero are left at zero), then multiplied by the bound factor
$g_\Phi$, evaluated pointwise from the same running field with the
buffer width $b_{\Phi,k}(z)$ above --- the taper is applied after the
normalization, so that proximity to a bound reduces a level's total
amplitude rather than redistributing it --- and finally by
$C_{\Phi,k}(z)$ and $S_k$, giving the amplitude field $A_\Phi$.

The amplitudes are then convolved with the turbulon envelope. The 3D
Mexican hat of the main-text appendix ($3 - \rho$ form) is constructed
with $\sigma = k/\pi$, isotropic in grid-index space (the grid's
vertical compression realizes the stretched metric of the main-text
appendix), truncated at five turbulon sizes from the center along each
axis, and shifted to zero mean over the truncated support (removing the
small truncation residual of the analytically zero 3D integral). If the kernel exceeds
the field extent horizontally it is periodically folded onto the field
period. $A_\Phi$ is convolved with this kernel --- circular in $x$ and
$y$, zero-padded in $z$ --- and the result is added to
$\widetilde{\Phi}$.

Finally, each scalar is projected onto its bounds while preserving the
horizontal mean at every level: $\Phi \rightarrow \min(\max(\Phi - \mu,
\Phi_{\min}), \Phi_{\max})$, with $\mu$ solved per level such that the
horizontal mean equals its value before the projection. The projected
mean is a continuous, piecewise-linear, monotonically decreasing
function of $\mu$, so the root is found exactly by sorting the
breakpoints $\Phi_i - \Phi_{\max}$ and $\Phi_i - \Phi_{\min}$, or to
arbitrary precision by bisection. The correction is folded into the
current class's perturbation field, so that the gradients seen by
subsequent classes are those of the projected field.

\subsection{Final fields}

After the finest class ($k = 2dx$), the output fields are $h = \langle
h \rangle_t + \widetilde{h}$ and $q_t = \langle q_t \rangle_t +
\widetilde{q_t}$; the per-class projection guarantees they lie within
the input bounds, so no final clip is applied. Temperature, water
phases, and pressure are then recovered by the saturation adjustment of
the main-text appendix.

\subsection{Nested refinement}

A completed simulation (or a previous nest) can be continued over a
subdomain to finer resolution: the nest runs the identical per-class
loop for the size classes below its parent's finest, down to twice the
nest's grid spacing. The nest inherits the parent's state --- the
scalar perturbations (parent field minus its mean profile) and the flux
field are extracted over the nest region and interpolated trilinearly
onto the nest's grids; nothing is re-seeded from scratch. The
amplitude ladder is likewise inherited: the parent's finest-class
$C_{\Phi,k}(z)$, scaled by $(k/k_{\mathrm{parent}})^{H_h}$ and
interpolated to each nest class's vertical grid, extends the single
ladder anchored at the root outer scale with no discontinuity at the
overlap scale. The flux requires no special handling: because the
per-class renormalization restores the volume mean the field entered
with, a nest keeps the regional flux anomaly it inherited.

The extraction carries a halo on each non-spanning horizontal side,
of per-class physical width equal to the turbulon support at that
class, so that turbulons centered outside the nest still reach into it.
The halo is context, not domain: it is excluded from every statistic
evaluated over the simulated domain --- the advective-weight
normalization, the mean absolute multiplier noise, the flux volume
means, and the mean-preserving projection --- and it is discarded on
output. (Bounds are still enforced across the halo, since the next
class reads gradients and the bound taper from it.) Along any axis the
nest spans entirely, the parent's periodicity (or aperiodicity) is
preserved with the parent extent as the period and no halo; a nest
whose halo would cross a non-periodic parent boundary is rejected. The
vertical is always aperiodic: the halo is clipped at the ground and
domain top, and turbulon centers may lie outside the nest's vertical
range (anywhere between the domain surface and top), with their tails
reaching in.

Each nest's random seed is derived deterministically from the parent's
seed and the output group name unless given explicitly, and the
per-class seed spawning is as in a root simulation. Output is written
as a NetCDF group inside the parent's file with world-absolute
coordinates, recording its parent group for recursive refinement; an
elevated nest's basal pressure is taken from the parent's
three-dimensional pressure field.

\section{Complete specification of the LES-ensemble comparison}
\label{ssect:rcemip analysis}

This section specifies, in full, the analysis behind the comparison of
STEAM to the RCEMIP cloud-resolving ensemble
\citep[][]{wing2018,wing2020}. The intent is that the comparison could be
reproduced exactly from this description alone. The comparison is
against the elongated-channel (\texttt{RCE\_large300}) configurations of
nine models, used for the profile, distribution, and
correlation-dimension statistics; object size distributions, for which
the channel geometry is too narrow, are instead computed from a
wide-domain STEAM ensemble with no direct hydrodynamic counterpart, as
described below.

\subsection{Comparison data}
\label{ssect:rcemip data}

Nine models provide usable
three-dimensional snapshot output for the 300~K channel configuration:
SAM, CM1, three Met Office Unified Model variants (CASIM, RA1-T,
RA1-T-nocloud), SCALE, UCLA-CRM, and two ICON variants (LEM and NWP).
Domains are channels of approximately $6000\times 400$~km$^2$ at 3~km
horizontal spacing ($1984$--$2048$ cells long, $128$--$144$ cells wide),
with model-specific stretched vertical grids ($74$--$98$ levels to
$33$--$39.5$~km). Each model supplies three snapshots separated by ten
days (days 80, 90, and 100 of the simulation; days 93, 103, and 113 for
the UM variants; days 80, 90, and 99 for ICON-LEM). Two archived models
are excluded: ICON-AES, because its output contains neither a height
coordinate nor pressure, so moist static energy cannot be constructed;
and MESONH, because its archived three-dimensional specific humidity is
a documented data error in the RCEMIP archive (Known RCEMIP Bugs
document, Sect.~17: the field is ``much too small in the lower
troposphere'' by a height-dependent factor), while its column-integrated
water and remaining fields are physically normal --- the corruption is
in the archived field, not the simulation, but it contaminates every
vapor-dependent statistic used here.

All humidities are converted to mixing
ratios before any further computation. Most models archive specific
quantities (mass per unit moist air, CMOR names \texttt{hus},
\texttt{clw}, \texttt{cli}); these are converted as $r = q/(1-q)$, with
the vapor-only denominator (the neglected condensate contribution to
moist-air mass changes $r$ by ${\lesssim}2\%$ where condensate is
largest). SAM archives vapor as a mixing ratio directly. Cloud liquid
and ice are taken from \texttt{clw} and \texttt{cli} (UCLA names ice
\texttt{ice}); precipitating condensate is excluded everywhere, because
several models do not archive it.

Total water is $q_t = r_v + r_c + r_i$ and moist static energy is
$h = c_p T + g z + L_v r_v$ with $c_p = 1004$~J\,kg$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$,
$g = 9.81$~m\,s$^{-2}$, $L_v = 2.5\times 10^6$~J\,kg$^{-1}$ (STEAM's
constants, applied identically to every model).

Two archives require special
handling. (i)~The ICON files carry only a level \emph{index} (top-down)
as their vertical coordinate; physical height is recovered by inverting
the frozen moist static energy field distributed alongside them,
$z = (f_{\mathrm{mse}} - 1004.64\,T - 2500800\,q_v + 333700\,q_i)/9.80665$
(the constants of that archive, used only for this recovery), averaged
horizontally at one snapshot; the level-wise horizontal standard
deviation of the recovered height is required to be below 50~m.
(ii)~UCLA-CRM stores height as the fastest dimension and includes a
below-ground ghost level, which is dropped. Where a model archives no
pressure (SCALE, UCLA-CRM), the RCEMIP analytic-sounding surface
pressure of 1014.8~hPa is used to initialize STEAM; otherwise the
horizontal mean of the model's lowest-level pressure is used. All
reductions of single-precision fields use double-precision accumulators.

\subsection{STEAM configuration}
\label{ssect:rcemip steam config}

One deliberately untuned configuration is used throughout; nothing is
calibrated to any individual host. The spheroscale profile decreases
linearly from 100~m at the surface to 1~m at the domain top, with
turbulons isotropic below it
(\texttt{piecewise\_isotropic\_below\_spheroscale}); the horizontal Hurst
exponent is $H_h = 0.45$ and the vertical-anisotropy exponent
$H_z = 5/9$; turbulons use the Mexican-hat envelope; one scale class per
octave ($n_c = 1$). The turbulon flux component is active: flux
increments drawn from extremal ($\beta = -1$) L\'evy noise with $\alpha
= 1.8$ and noise amplitude $c = 0.143$, targeting a realized flux
intermittency of $C_1 \approx 0.05$ through the approximate calibration
$C_1 = 1.674\,c^{1.8}$.

For the channel comparison STEAM runs on a $2048\times 128$ grid at
$\Delta x = 3$~km ($6144\times 384$~km$^2$, matching the host geometry
class) with outer scale $L = 96$~km, giving five scale classes (96, 48,
24, 12, 6~km). The domain is periodic; the 384~km cross-channel extent
holds four outer-scale tiles. The simulated depth is 20~km.

Each STEAM run is initialized from one host snapshot: the host's
horizontal-mean $h(z)$ and $q_t(z)$, interpolated to a uniform 50~m
profile grid, plus its surface pressure as above. The scalar bounds are
anchored to the prescribed 300~K sea surface temperature, which every
realization sees: $q_t \in [0,\ q_{\mathrm{sat}}(300\,\mathrm{K},
p_s)]$, since no parcel can acquire more vapor than saturated contact
with the sea surface provides, and $h \in [\min_z \langle h \rangle(z)
- c_p \Delta T_{\max},\ c_p \cdot 300\,\mathrm{K} + L_v\,
q_{\mathrm{sat}}(300\,\mathrm{K}, p_s)]$, where the upper bound is the
saturation moist static energy at the surface and the lower bound
allows a maximum temperature deficit of $\Delta T_{\max} = 10$~K below
the coldest point of the mean profile. One realization is run per host
snapshot (three per model), with a distinct seed per snapshot, so host
and STEAM sample counts match by construction.

% prescription agreed today; the archived runs used the older ad hoc
% bounds (h: min-10cp/max+10cp; qt: max(0.03, 1.5*max qt)) and must be
% regenerated under the new algorithm before final figures anyway.
% Ditto (later same day): archived runs also predate the removal of the
% flux substeps (they ran 4 substeps/class), the 3D Mexican hat (3-rho)
% envelope, the removal of flux from W, and S_k as the single per-class
% noise; the C1 = 1.674 c^1.8 calibration was fit under the old config
% and needs re-fitting before c is chosen for the regenerated runs.

For size distributions and
large-domain fractal dimensions, nine additional STEAM runs use a
$2048\times 1024$ grid at $\Delta x = 3$~km ($6144\times 3072$~km$^2$),
one per host profile (the first snapshot of each host), with the same
configuration otherwise and distinct seeds. These runs have no direct
hydrodynamic counterpart; they exist because the channel geometry
cannot constrain size-distribution exponents (Sect.~\ref{ssect:rcemip
fractal}).

\subsection{Profile statistics and anomaly distributions}
\label{ssect:rcemip stats}

Per-level statistics are computed on each case's native vertical grid:
horizontal mean and variance of $h$ and $q_t$, and cloud fraction
defined as the areal fraction with non-precipitating condensate
$q_c + q_i > 0.01$~g\,kg$^{-1}$. For comparison across grids all
profiles are linearly interpolated to a common 100~m grid spanning
0--20~km (levels outside a model's native range are excluded, not
extrapolated), and snapshot-averaged.

Differences are presented as $\Delta = $ STEAM $-$ host, one curve per
model. The yardstick shading is built from the \emph{pairwise}
inter-host differences: for each level, all ordered pairs
host$_i$ $-$ host$_j$ ($i \neq j$; 72 pairs for nine models) are formed
from the snapshot-averaged profiles, and the shading shows their full
range and interquartile (25--75\%) range. Pairwise differences, rather
than deviations from the ensemble mean, are the like-for-like reference
for a pairwise quantity such as $\Delta$; deviations from the mean are
smaller by roughly $\sqrt{2}$ and would overstate STEAM's exceedances.

Anomaly probability densities are computed at the model level nearest
4~km and 10~km: at each level the horizontal mean of that snapshot is
subtracted, all snapshots of a case are pooled, and the pooled sample is
histogrammed in 150 equal-width bins spanning the pooled sample's full
range (no percentile capping, which would truncate the tails that are
precisely the point of the comparison), normalized to a density.

\subsection{Optical-depth masks, correlation dimension, and size
distributions}
\label{ssect:rcemip fractal}

Cloud masks are built from vertically integrated optical depth computed
from the liquid and ice water paths with the SAM bulk-optics
relationships (as implemented in the \texttt{cloudyview} package);
precipitating species are excluded as above. A column is cloudy where
$\tau > 1$. Masks are binary arrays on each case's native horizontal
grid; the three masks of a case are always analyzed together as one
ensemble, never averaged one-at-a-time.

The ensemble correlation dimension $D_2$ is computed with
\texttt{objscale} \citep{dewitt2026acp} using the sandbox estimator at
R\'enyi order $q = 2$ on the one-sided edge set of the masks --- the
Grassberger--Procaccia correlation integral --- with pair separations
binned between a minimum of two pixels (6~km) and a maximum of one-third
the narrow domain extent (${\approx}127$~km); sandbox centers are
subsampled by a factor of ten. The two-pixel channel minimum admits
pixel-scale discretization bias at the small end of the range and is the
price of retaining one decade of scaling in a 128-cell-wide mask. The
correlation integrals themselves are inspected for linearity in log--log
space before the fitted slope is reported as a dimension.

Object size distributions use the truncation-corrected estimator of
\citet{dewitt2024acp}: objects are 4-connected components of the mask;
areas (and summed perimeters) are binned logarithmically; bins in which
more than half the objects touch the domain boundary, or which hold
fewer than 30 objects, are excluded; the power-law exponent is the
least-squares slope of $\log_{10}$ counts against $\log_{10}$ size over
the surviving bins. Domains are periodic, but boundary-touching objects
are treated as truncated rather than wrapped --- a conservative choice
that discards, rather than reconnects, objects crossing the seam. For
the channel geometry the surviving range is only ${\approx}1.5$ decades
of area and the nine hosts' fitted exponents disagree by a factor of
four (0.6--2.3), so the channel ensemble does not constrain the exponent
at this domain size --- consistent with the finite-domain analysis of
\citet{dewitt2024acp}. Size distributions are therefore computed from
the wide-domain STEAM ensemble (all nine masks analyzed at once), along
with the individual fractal dimension (filled perimeter against filled
area) and the ensemble correlation dimension at the same estimator
settings, with sandbox centers subsampled by a factor of one hundred.
